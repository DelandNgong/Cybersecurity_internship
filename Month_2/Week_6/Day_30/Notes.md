Day 30: Data Protection & Handling Sensitive Data

Core Concept: Data is a critical organizational asset. Data protection is a continuous cycle (a lifecycle) aimed at securing this asset from threats through a structured, holistic approach involving People, Processes, and Technology (PPT).

The Data Protection Lifecycle:

Govern:

Purpose: Establish the strategic foundation for data protection.

Activities: Creating data protection policies, classifying data based on sensitivity (e.g., Public, Internal, Confidential), creating a data catalog (an inventory of what data exists and where), and ensuring data resilience (backup and recovery plans).

Discover:

Purpose: Identify what sensitive data you have and where it resides.

Activities: Scanning systems, databases, and networks to locate sensitive information like Personal Identifiable Information (PII), financial records, and intellectual property.

Protect:

Purpose: Implement controls to shield data from unauthorized access and exposure.

Activities: Encrypting data at rest and in transit, managing encryption keys, enforcing access controls (principle of least privilege), and maintaining secure backups.

Comply:

Purpose: Ensure adherence to legal and regulatory requirements.

Activities: Generating compliance reports, enforcing data retention policies, and adhering to standards.

Detect:

Purpose: Identify potential threats and breaches in a timely manner.

Activities: Continuous monitoring of data access, using analytics and User and Entity Behavior Analytics (UEBA) to spot anomalies, and setting up security alerts.

Respond:

Purpose: Take action to contain and mitigate the impact of a security incident.

Activities: Creating incident cases, following dynamic playbooks, and using Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response (SOAR) tools for automated and manual response actions.

Key Takeaway: The outcome of the "Respond" phase often leads back to "Govern," creating a continuous improvement loop for the organization's data protection strategy.
